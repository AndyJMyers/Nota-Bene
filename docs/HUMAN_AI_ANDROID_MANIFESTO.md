# The Human–AI Android Manifesto

We are discovering better ways to make personal software by making it together, installing it on real devices and learning from what happens next.

Through 32 Visualisations, Nota Bene and the mistakes between them, we have come to value:

## Purpose and taste over generated possibility

## Working software on the phone over convincing software in the chat

## Short human–AI feedback loops over exhaustive advance specification

## Preserved milestones over heroic reconstruction

## Honest behaviour over impressive claims

## Personal agency over unnecessary dependence

The things on the right can be useful. The things on the left decide whether the app deserves to exist.

## Our principles

1. **Begin with a real human want.**  
   The strongest specification is something a person genuinely wishes existed and expects to use.

2. **Give the human the final word on meaning and taste.**  
   AI can propose, implement and analyse. It cannot replace the lived judgement that says whether an interaction feels clear, dignified, delightful or wrong.

3. **Give the AI room to finish bounded work.**  
   Once purpose and scope are clear, let the AI inspect, implement, test, document and package the result without turning every reversible detail into a meeting.

4. **Put a build in the human’s hand early.**  
   A rough APK on the intended phone teaches more than a polished description of an imaginary application.

5. **Treat the device as the court of final appeal.**  
   A control that looks elegant in source code may be awkward under a thumb. An animated wave may become a stationary boomerang. Reality gets the casting vote.

6. **Change one meaningful thing, then look again.**  
   Small, coherent increments keep cause and effect visible. They make taste actionable instead of abstract.

7. **Keep the path from change to installation short.**  
   Predictable development filenames, direct download links and repeatable builds protect the feedback loop from ceremony.

8. **Name and preserve moments of genuine progress.**  
   Commits, tags and releases are the project’s memory. Human-readable milestones make it possible to recover not only code, but intention.

9. **Build the simplest complete circuit.**  
   Enter it. Keep it. Show it back. Remind when necessary. Export when asked. A narrow feature that completes its promise is worth more than a broad façade.

10. **Let use reveal the roadmap.**  
    Daily friction outranks speculative feature lists. The next feature should usually emerge from the last real test.

11. **Keep data under the user’s control.**  
    Store locally when practical, export in ordinary formats and make backup, deletion and sharing deliberate and understandable.

12. **Do not confuse a demonstration with a dependable feature.**  
    Speech recognition that worked once, OCR that saw one receipt and a button that usually responds are evidence of direction—not completion.

13. **Make documentation tell the same truth as the app.**  
    README files, release notes, store listings, privacy statements and screenshots are part of the product. Aspirations must be labelled as aspirations.

14. **Prepare trust work before it becomes an emergency.**  
    Privacy, safety, accessibility and store compliance are easier to do honestly while the architecture is still understandable.

15. **Design for dignity, not a demographic stereotype.**  
    Clear controls, readable text and forgiving workflows benefit older users without presuming that age has extinguished taste, humour or aesthetic appetite.

16. **Keep seriousness and pleasure in the same machine.**  
    A useful app may also glow, twinkle, snow or send a square-rigger around the Horn. Delight is not clutter when it supports the desire to return.

17. **Make disagreement productive.**  
    The human should say “that is a boomerang, not a wave.” The AI should explain what happened, discard a failed abstraction and try again without defensiveness.

18. **Stop at a safe harbour.**  
    At the end of a working session, build, verify, commit, push and leave the documentation in order. Tomorrow’s collaboration should begin with the project intact.

## The compact working cycle

> Imagine together.  
> Build a small truth.  
> Put it on the phone.  
> Look without sentimentality.  
> Improve what matters.  
> Preserve what worked.  
> Repeat.

This is not automation replacing authorship. It is authorship gaining an extraordinarily capable workshop.
